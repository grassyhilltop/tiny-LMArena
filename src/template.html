Why ECI

One scale for every model since 2023

The Epoch Capabilities Index, built by Epoch AI, combines results from more than 50 benchmarks into a single scale of general capability. It stitches benchmarks together wherever models were tested on more than one, which sets each benchmark's difficulty. A model scores higher when it does better on harder benchmarks. Because old and new benchmarks are linked, a 2023 model and a 2026 model sit on the same scale even after individual benchmarks saturate. The methodology is public, and each score comes with a credible interval, except the two models that anchor the scale (Claude 3.5 Sonnet = 130, GPT-5 = 150).

That makes it a good yardstick for a long-run trend. A single benchmark can be saturated, leaked into training data or tuned for. A composite of many benchmarks dilutes any one benchmark's quirks, and Epoch runs many evaluations itself rather than relying on developers' own reports. It does not remove tuning: Epoch notes that developers can still optimize for the benchmarks it uses, and that open-weight models may do so more aggressively, so the gap to the frontier shown here may be understated.

It is also the index forecasters already use. Anthropic's Frontier Red Team, in “AI models may be capable of self-replicating without access to a datacenter” by William Zhou and Daniel Freeman (12 October 2026), plotted notable open-weight models of 30B total parameters or fewer on the ECI. In their simplified evaluation GLM-5.2 completed end-to-end replication, so they took it as a rough capability floor and projected when small models reach it, noting that the ECI is only a proxy for replication ability. The hero chart on this page extends their figure and updates it every day.

Think in thresholds and trend lines. Pick a capability bar that matters, draw the trend of the best models in a class, and read off when the trend crosses the bar, with an honest range. When new models arrive, the forecast updates. Labs, policymakers and builders can then plan for a capability before it shows up.

Two caveats. The ECI is a general index, so it is only a rough proxy for any one skill such as replication or coding; Anthropic makes the same point. And Epoch re-estimates scores as it adds benchmarks: GLM-5.2 was at when Anthropic wrote the post and is at today. This page therefore tracks each threshold by its reference model, not a frozen number.

Why single-GPU models. My research focuses on small language models. My hypothesis is that capable models will increasingly run offline: air-gapped on a single GPU, at the edge, as domain experts that keep data private. This page is a helper visualization that snapshots how fast this class of model improves and how far it trails the frontier. — Joel Sadler

Method

What counts as a single-GPU model, and how the forecast works

Definition. A model qualifies if its weights are open and, quantized to 4 bits, fill at most 75% of a 32 GB GPU such as an RTX 5090, leaving the rest for context. At an idealized half a byte per parameter that is at most 48B total parameters; mixture-of-experts models count all experts. Real 4-bit files run 5–20% larger, so models near the cap leave less room for context. On a 32 GB Mac, macOS lets the GPU use only about two-thirds of memory by default, so the practical cap there is lower. Any quantization is allowed in practice; 4-bit is the reference because quality holds up there and quantization-aware releases target it. So Flint (39.8B) counts, and a 120B model squeezed to 2 bits does not. It must also have an ECI score.

Anthropic's class. The “Models” switch under Options approximates the narrower class in Anthropic's chart. They used 30B total parameters or fewer; this page uses ≤ B so that Gemma 4 31B, which their chart shows, is included. Their published fit was 13.7 ECI per year (R² 0.95), and the post does not state the fit window. With today's scores this class gives fitted from July 2023 and over the last 24 months, so the window matters at least as much as Epoch's re-estimates.

Trend. A model is best at its release if it beat every earlier single-GPU model on the day it came out. The line is an ordinary least-squares fit of ECI against release date over those models, from the start of the chosen window. The shaded band is the 90% prediction interval for a new best-at-release model.

Crossing dates. The projected date is where the line meets the threshold; the range is where the edges of the band meet it. “Crossed” means an actual model scored at or above the threshold.

Regions. “Models from” uses the country of the developer's organization in Epoch's data: US, China, or the rest of the world (other countries and cross-border collaborations). It filters the top 3, the chart, the key numbers, the tables and the gap analysis; the trend is refitted to the best model at each date within the group.

Thresholds. Self-replication floor: GLM-5.2's current ECI. Frontier one year ago: the best ECI of any model released by this date last year. Today's frontier: the best ECI of any model today.

Data and updates. A GitHub Action pulls Epoch's ECI scores and model metadata every day. Parameter counts come from Epoch, then Hugging Face. The code, data (JSON, CSV, daily history) and curated mappings are on GitHub.
